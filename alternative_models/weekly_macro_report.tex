\section*{Methodology}

\subsection*{Framework}
For each target variable, we run a systematic model search: 5 models $\times$ 4 feature sets = 20 combinations, evaluated on a hard train/test split (train: pre-2023, test: 2023--2025). COVID months (2020-02 to 2020-08) excluded from training. Only models passing the overfitting gate (test RMSE $< 1.5\times$ CV RMSE) qualify for inference.

\subsection*{Feature Construction}
All features are strictly \textbf{lagged} (no contemporaneous information). High-frequency predictors (daily: oil, VIX, yields, FX; weekly: claims) are aggregated to monthly via MIDAS Beta polynomial weights ($\theta_1=1, \theta_2=5$, K=22 for daily, K=4 for weekly). Same-frequency regressors enter with 1-month lag.

\subsection*{Multicollinearity Treatment}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{StandardScaler}: all features standardised to zero mean, unit variance
    \item \textbf{VIF elimination}: iteratively drop highest-VIF feature until all VIF $<$ 10
    \item \textbf{PCA}: fit on train-only (ex-COVID), extract 3--5 orthogonal factors, \textbf{lag by 1 month}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Principal Component Eigenstructure}
PCA is fitted on the standardised lagged predictor universe using the training sample excluding COVID. Each principal component is an eigenvector of the predictors' covariance matrix, and its eigenvalue measures the variance explained by that component. The retained PCs therefore represent orthogonal macro channels ordered by their eigenvalues.

\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=1.2em]
    \item \textbf{PC1}: the largest eigenvalue, capturing the broad monetary policy / yield regime channel.
    \item \textbf{PC2}: the second-largest eigenvalue, capturing curve slope and demand-pressure dynamics.
    \item \textbf{PC3}: the third component, capturing energy / supply-side pressure and real activity momentum.
    \item \textbf{PC4}: a confidence / wealth/income channel with lower but meaningful variance.
    \item \textbf{PC5}: a financial stress / risk-premium channel, the smallest retained eigenvalue among the five.
\end{itemize}

The regression coefficients on PC1--PC5 are therefore best read as sensitivities to these orthogonal eigenvector channels rather than individual raw predictors.

\subsection*{Model Specifications}
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=1.2em]
    \item \textbf{OLS}: unpenalised linear regression (baseline)
    \item \textbf{Ridge}: L2 penalty, $\lambda$ by LOO-CV --- shrinks correlated coefficients uniformly
    \item \textbf{ElasticNet}: L1+L2 mix, automatic feature selection via sparsity
    \item \textbf{XGBoost}: gradient-boosted trees (depth 2--4, L2=2--5, lr=0.03--0.05)
\end{itemize}

Coefficients shown in this report are taken directly from the backend results file and represent standardised linear weights for each selected model. They are used to identify the dominant macro channels and to explain the economic drivers behind each forecast.

\subsection*{Probabilistic Output}
\textbf{Point forecast:} best model refitted on full ex-COVID sample.
\textbf{Confidence intervals:} Quantile Regression at $\tau \in \{0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90\}$.
\textbf{Beat/miss:} Ensemble of Ordered Logit (25\%) + XGBoost Classifier (50\%) + Quantile Regression (25\%).

\subsection*{Cross-Variable Coherence}

The four forecasts paint a coherent macro picture:

\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=1.2em]
    \item \textbf{CPI below consensus + PPI above consensus} ($\rightarrow$ margin squeeze): CPI at 2.58\% vs PPI at 0.96\% MoM suggests producer costs are rising faster than consumer prices --- a margin squeeze for corporates. This is consistent with the energy cost pass-through still working through the supply chain upstream (PPI) before reaching the consumer (CPI).
    \item \textbf{Retail inline} ($\rightarrow$ consumer absorbing shocks): The 0.37\% retail forecast is close to consensus, suggesting the consumer is neither collapsing (which would be deflationary) nor overheating (which would add demand-pull pressure). This validates the ``soft landing'' narrative.
    \item \textbf{UK unemployment drifting higher} ($\rightarrow$ BoE dilemma deepens): At 5.13\%, UK unemployment is rising while CPI remains elevated (3.3\% YoY in March). This is the textbook stagflationary signal: the BoE cannot cut to support employment without risking re-igniting inflation, and cannot hike without deepening the labour market deterioration.
    \item \textbf{Global spillover:} A below-consensus US CPI would be \textit{dovish} for the Fed (supporting rate cuts), which would compress US yields and --- via our VAR spillover analysis --- reduce pressure on gilt yields (29\% of UK Level FEVD from US). This would partially offset the hawkish domestic signal from rising UK unemployment.
\end{itemize}
